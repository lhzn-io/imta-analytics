conducted with 649 consumers in the New York City area who

The study explored attitudes toward IMTA and seafood culti-

Support for IMTA

About 34% of the respondents initially expressed a positive
attitude toward farmed seafood, 48% were indifferent, and the
remainder had a negative attitude. Many consumers acknowl-
edged they were not familiar with the terminology of integrated
multitrophic aquaculture or polyculture.

When the IMTA concept was explained in relatively basic
terms, 88% of the seafood consumers expressed support, either
completely (16%) or mostly (72%), of the use of IMTA techniques
in the aquaculture industry. Even among those with a negative
opinion of farmed seafood, 47% reported feeling more positive
toward fish, shellfish and seaweed grown using an IMTA approach.
The study also asked consumers to compare IMTA-raised sea-
food with seafood raised in monoculture systems on a number of
key product attributes.

Consumers are receptive to IMTA seafood such as these extra-meaty
steamed mussel appetizers served at the Rossmount Inn
in St. Andrews, New Brunswick, Canada. Photo by T. Chopin.

Integrated multitrophic aquaculture (IMTA) combines the
cultivation of fed aquaculture species such as finfish with organic
extractive aquaculture species like suspension and deposit feed-
ers and inorganic extractive aquaculture species like seaweeds.
The IMTA concept aims for balanced ecosystem management
that provides environmental sustainability, economic stability
and societal acceptability.

Through IMTA, some of the food, nutrients and by-products

considered lost in finfish monoculture are captured and con-
verted into food, fertilizers and energy to produce extractive
crops of commercial value while biomitigation takes place. In
this way, all the cultivation components have potential economic
value, as well as key roles in the services and recycling processes
of the engineered ecosystem.

Summary:
A survey of New York seafood consumers found a preference for integrated multitrophic aquaculture over mono-
culture, once the IMTA concept was explained. IMTA seafood was considered better for the environment and animal
welfare and, to a lesser degree, safer and healthier. Regarding quality, freshness and taste, IMTA seafood outper-
formed conventional seafood, although many consumers did not recognize quality differences. Monoculture-raised
seafood was considered more readily available. Price was a major factor in consumer preferences.

improved meat yield. Using visuals in the online survey to illus-

higher and lower yield on a variety of product attributes.

In terms of marketing IMTA-raised fresh mussels, the more
obvious advantages to consumers are likely to be centered more
on the improved environmental benefits and less on the physical
attributes of the mussels. Improved meat yield, better value and
more appealing appearance may be the most relevant attributes
to communicate to potential buyers when promoting higher-yield
IMTA-raised mussels.

Ecolabel/Price

Finally, the study explored the impacts an ecolabel could
have on consumers’ decisions to buy IMTA-raised fresh mussels.
Consumers often report a willingness to buy a new or improved
product that offers environmental benefits if it is readily available
where they shop at the same price as other offerings. Since IMTA-
raised mussels will likely have a limited distribution network and
require a price premium compared to conventionally grown fresh
mussels, how will consumers react to this reality?

While 41% of seafood consumers indicated they would buy
ecofriendly seafood as often as they could if available where they
shopped, only 24% said they would probably and 7% would defi-
nitely make a specific trip to buy ecolabel food. As a result, the
limited distribution channels for IMTA-raised mussels will nega-
tively impact potential buying intentions, even among supporters.
To better understand if IMTA-raised fresh mussels can com-
mand a price premium compared to conventionally raised mus-
sels, a penetration analysis was performed starting with the 649

Effective Marketing

New York seafood consumers clearly perceived positive envi-
ronmental benefits from IMTA practices in the aquaculture indus-
try. To advance the sales of fresh mussels raised in IMTA systems,
however, the considerable marketing challenge will be to educate
consumers on their product benefits.

Price was found to have twice the weight of such other factors

as an ecolabel and higher meat yield in the consumers’ purchase
decisions. Solid marketing plans that include a sound pricing
strategy and strong channel distribution, coupled with an effective
educational component, will be critical to develop a sustainable
consumer retail market for IMTA-raised fresh mussels.
