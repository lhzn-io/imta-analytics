Abstract. The growth of conventional aquaculture has created environmental issues due
to  excessive  feeding,  low  dissolved  oxygen  level  etc  into  the  water  body.  Organic
aquaculture is a recent development as a solution to these issues. It is a clean alternative
to  reduce  pollution  and  to  produce  safer  consumable  food.  In  the  past  few  decades,
recirculating aquaculture systems (RAS) were introduced to maintain pond water quality
through lesser water exchange, by focusing on water reuse after treatment. In this study,
the  technical  viability  of  RAS  was  analyzed  for  introduction  in  organic  aquaculture
systems.  Indian major carps  (IMCs) were cultured through conventional methods in the
three  tanks  of  conventional  system,  while  the  same  species  was  grown  organically  in
another three tanks in organic system. The stocking density and physical conditions were
kept  same  for  both  cultured  systems.  The  RAS  consisted  of  fish  culture  tank  with  an
average volume of each tank was 165 m3, a screen filter, foam fractionator and trickling
filter.  The  various  water  quality  parameters,  i.e.,  solid  size  distribution  in  water,  and
removal efficiencies in biological treatment of both systems were compared. The present
study,  particle  size  distribution  of  solids  in  water  body  was  measured  by  filtration,
followed by weighing of dry residue solids. Pore sizes of 1000μ, 100μ, 20μ and 3μ were
employed for filtration. For organic tanks, a majority of solids are of size between 1 mm
and 100 μm. The larger sized particles (> 30 μm) constitute nearly 70 % of the solids and
the trait differs from the generalized conclusions of that a major part of sediments will be
of  sizes  less  than  20  μm.  For  the  conventional  tanks,  majority  of  solids  are  of  size
between 3 mm and 20 μm and TAN shows higher removal efficiencies for effluents from
organic  system  rather  than  conventional  system.  The  results  showed  that  organic
aquaculture causes lesser pollution load per weight of fish. The particle size distribution
of organic water was better compatible to screening and sedimentation than conventional
water.  Also  the  filtration  efficiencies  in  nitrifying  trickling  filters  of  both  water  bodies
were  comparable,  with  that  of  organic  water  slightly  on  the  higher  side.  Thus
recirculating water treatment systems are introduced in organic aquaculture.

1.  Introduction
Aquaculture  has  gained  global  importance  because  of  its  contribution  towards  food  and  nutritional
security,  employment  generation  and  economic  upliftment  of rural  peoples [1,2]    and  foreign  exchange
earnings. Traditional or conventional aquaculture uses fertilizers and commercial feed which causes the
major  source  of  deterioration  of  water  quality  and  associated  with  water  pollution  which  increase  the
mortality rate of fishes [3] . Large amount of clean water needs to be exchanged in conventional methods
to  maintain  the  ambient  water  quality  [4,5]  .Therefore;  it  should  need  to  treat  aquaculture  water  in  a
proper manner. The exchanged water pollutes open water sources when allowed to flow without required
treatment.  Hence,  recirculating  aquaculture  systems  (RAS)  may  offer  an  alternative  method  to  tank
aquaculture technology [6,7] . The RAS have more advantages over the pond aquaculture system [8]. The
RAS increases the production capacity with the limited resources and water supply [9]. Thus reduces the
environmental impact [10] and diseases of fish [11]. In recent years, it has significantly reduces land and
water  demands,  also  increases  the  yield  of  fishes  [12].  The  RAS  signify  the  comparatively  new
technology with a wide deviation in system design and quality available [13] .

      In  recent  days,  a  very  popular  and  alternative  way  is  organic  aquaculture  [14].  The  organic
aquaculture is an innovative concept which can act as an alternative of conventional aquaculture method
[15] . Apart from reducing the rate of deterioration of water quality, it can also deliver healthy and safe
food products [16]. The principles of organic aquaculture also encourage alternatives to conventional fish
feed  constituents  of  fish  meal  and  fish  oil  to  reduce  the  stress  on  global  fish  stocks  [17,18].  Since  the
pollution  load  per  weight  of  fish  is  less,  higher  stocking  densities  can  be  employed  to  maximize
commercial returns [19]. But higher water exchange becomes necessary at higher stocking densities.

     Incredible increased organic fish demand in globally [20] but production is very less due to maintained
proper  organic  regulation  as  well  as  stocking  density  is  very  less  [21],  this  is  not  complete  protein
necessity  of  fresh  fish,  hygienic  fish  appreciated  consumer  [22].  One  solution  to  these  problems  is
integrated  organic  and  RAS  to  improve  the  water  quality  and  fish  production  with  higher  density.  The
controlled environment makes it easier to keep diseases out and avoid drug applications [23]. RAS require
very less addition of fresh water and thus very less discharge of untreated water to open sources [24]. But
being  capital  intensive,  an  adaptation  of  RAS  for  organic  aquaculture  requires  assurances  about  its
technical viability and economic feasibility. In the present research to develop a recirculatory aquaculture
system  (RAS)  for  organic  aquaculture  with  respect  to  solid  size  distribution  and  ammonia  removal
efficiency  and  compares  it  with  conventional  aquaculture.  The  specific  objectives  of  the  study  are  as
follows:  (i)  To  study  about  the  fish  husbandry;  (ii)  To  investigate  the  nutrients  removal  and
physicochemical  parameters  analysis;  and  (iii)  finally  to calculated the fish  growth  parameters.  Finally,
integrated the organic aquaculture and recirculating aquaculture system (RAS) for sustainable production
of Indian major carp (IMCs).

2. Methodology

2.1 Fish husbandry and Experimental condition

The  experimental  organic  and  conventional  systems  were  shown  in  Figure  1.  Average  volume  of  each
tank was 165 m3. The experiments were conducted for 6 months of period with Indian Major Carps (IMC)
species  with  a  ratio  of  4:3:3  catla  (Catlacatla,  Hamilton),  rohu  (Labeorohita,  Hamilton),  and  mrigal
(Cirrihinusmrigala, Hamilton), fingerlings were collected from local hatchery and free form genetically
modified organism. The initial stocking density was 3.0 kg/m3. After stocked to the culture tank the fishes
were acclimatized to the tank condition. The aeration was given to maintain the DO within 6.5 to 8.5mg/L
by using the diffuser aerator. The experiments were conducted with three conventional and three organic
tanks. Whereas the organic tanks were fed with vermin compost (12,443 kg ha-1) and vermi wash (93.33
L ha-1), the natural tanks were fertilized with cow dung (6653 kg ha-1), urea (31 kg ha-1), and Triple Super
Phosphate (TSP) (16 kg ha-1) [1,25]. Commercial fish feed containing fish meal and fish oil as the major
constituents was applied in conventional ponds. Organic feed was formulated with earth worm meal from
earth  worms  (Eiseniafetida),  organic  soybean  and  organic  maize  from  farms  of  Agricultural  and  Food
Engineering  Department  (On  Standards  [2001]  Organic  Aquaculture,  in  India)  and  pelleted  in  the
Aquaculture  Engineering  workshop,  IIT  Kharagpur.  The  crude  protein  content  of  both  the  feeds  was
maintained at 32 % and is presented in Table 1.

The RAS consisted of fish culture tank and average volume of each tank was 165 m3. The screen filter,

Water from the raising tank was first supplied to the foam fractionator via the first pump, and then three
trickling filters received additional water delivery from the second pump, which was utilized to transport
water  from  the  sump  to  the  rearing  tank.  The  RAS  system's  tiny  particles  and  dissolved  solids  are
typically removed using a foam fractionator. Air is delivered via an air pump to the diffuser air stone at
the base of the foam fractionator. The effluent was collected in a sump from the foam fractionator. The

Efficiency  in  biological  removal  systems  was  analyzed  using  pilot  scale  trickling  filters.  The  setup
consisted  of  three  acrylic  cylindrical  columns  (Figure  2),  with  90  mm  diameter  and  1800  mm  height.
Nylon pot scrubbers were used as media [26]. A free board of 30 cm was given at the top. The inlet to the
tower was through a shower and water was pumped with a 0.5 HP centrifugal pump to drain out through
the  bottom  outlet.  A  flow meter  and  valve  was  connected  so  as  to  control and measure  discharge.  Air-
stones  were  attached  at  regular  intervals  along  the  depth  to  ensure  uniform  and  adequate  aeration.  The
towers were given black covering to prevent algal growth. Initiation of the trickling filters was done by
inoculating  the  filter  media  with  a  culture  solution  of  required  chemicals  like  sodium  bicarbonate  and
ammonium  chloride,  manganese  sulphate,  ferric  chloride  etc.  The  towers  were  left  in  batch  mode  for
about  six  weeks  and  ammonia,  nitrite  and  nitrite  concentrations  were  regularly  monitored.  During  the
experiments, dissolved oxygen (DO) of waterbody above 5 mg/ L is maintain and pH in the range of 7-8.
After the presence of nitrogenous bacteria was confirmed, the efficiency of removal of Total Ammonia
Nitrogen (TAN) at various flow rates was calculated in both the towers. A submersible pump (0.37 kW)
was used to deliver the water from the sump.

2.4 Statistical analysis

One-way ANOVA was used to evaluate the data on the  various proximate composition of feed and fish
development  parameters,  with  the  conventional  recirculating  aquaculture  system  and  the  organic
recirculating  system  serving  as  the  factors.  Duncans's  new  multiple  test  was  used  for  post-hoc
comparisons in order to identify significant differences (p˂0.05) across treatments [26] .

The result shows from the Figure 5 that for organic ponds, a majority of solids are of size between 1 mm
and 100 μm. The larger sized particles (> 30 μm) constitute nearly 70 % of the solids and the trait differs
from the generalized conclusions of that a major part of pond sediments will be of sizes less than 20 μm.
This characteristic of organic aquaculture is of immense potential in RAS, because a much larger amount
of solids can be removed from the system with respect to conventional aquaculture through screening and
sedimentation  (Figure  6).  Since,  settling  velocity  increase  with  particle  size,  higher  sedimentation
efficiency can be obtained for a given surface overflow rate in sedimentation tanks. Also larger pore sizes
of screens can easily remove a greater proportion of solids.

Analysis  of  efficiencies  of  biological  removal  of  TAN  shows  higher  removal  efficiencies  for  effluents
from organic system. (Figure 7) The trend remained same in both batch and continuous mode of operation.
The results imply that organic aquaculture can be practiced economically in extensive and semi intensive
systems with minimum water exchange due to its better water quality. The higher dissolved oxygen can
eliminate  requirement  of  artificial  aeration.  Relatively  higher  stocking  densities  can  be  employed  in
intensive  systems  as  the  pollution  load  per  weight  of  fish  is  lower.  This  enhances  the  scope  of  better
commercial returns. The particle size distribution of solids in organic aquaculture waste water facilitates
better sedimentation. This coupled with better nutrient removal makes recirculatory aquaculture systems
highly compatible for organic aquaculture.

4. Conclusion

Organic  aquaculture  techniques  have  the  potential  to  create  good  quality  fishes  with  much  lesser
environmental effects. Organic RAS were operated organic aquaculture standards by using organic feed,
fertilizer  and  minimizing  the  use  of  synthetic  feed  supplements.  The  combination  of  RAS  and  Organic
aquaculture  would  be  produce  better  quality  of  fishes.  The  investment  involved  in  extensive  and  semi
intensive cultures will be less while intensive systems with higher stocking density offer better returns.
This study showed that organic aquaculture causes lesser pollution load per weight of fish. The particle
size distribution of organic water was better compatible to screening and sedimentation than conventional
water. Also, the filtration efficiencies in nitrifying trickling filters of both water bodies were comparable,
with that of organic water slightly on the higher side. Better sedimentation efficiency and higher nutrient
removal  can  be  obtained from  the  waste  water  of  organic aquaculture. This  makes  it  highly  feasible to
adapt recirculation aquaculture systems for organic aquaculture. We recommend that Indian major carp
production rate will be increased with integrated organic aquaculture and recirculating aquaculture system
(RAS).
